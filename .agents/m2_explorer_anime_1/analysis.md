## 1. Executive Summary & Architectural Scope

In Milestone 2 of the Anime Character Pipeline, Feature F04 (Universal Humanoid Bone Hierarchy) and Feature F05 (30-Phalange Articulated Fingers) establish the foundational skeletal deformation system for game-ready, VTuber, VRM, and cinematics workflows.

### 1.1 Core Problems Solved
1. **Universal Cross-Platform Rig Compatibility (F04)**: Anime characters must simultaneously support VRM 1.0 (VTuber/VSeeFace/UniVRM), MMD (MikuMikuDance motion retargeting), Blender Rigify, and Unity Humanoid without manual bone remapping. We establish an authoritative canonical bone naming scheme with direct $1:1$ equivalence mappings.
2. **Natural Finger Curling without Axis Twisting (F05)**: Traditional auto-rigging algorithms generate arbitrary bone rolls along finger phalanges, causing local rotation around any single axis to skew the fingers diagonally, penetrate adjacent phalanges, or twist the fingernails unnaturally. We formulate the exact mathematical bone roll alignment using the anatomical flexion plane normal ($\vec{v}_1 \times \vec{v}_2$), ensuring that a pure positive rotation around the local $X$-axis ($\Delta \theta_x > 0$) curls all 5 fingers inward into a tight, natural fist with zero out-of-plane torque.
3. **Dynamic Landmark Scaling ($1.45\text{m} - 1.75\text{m}$)**: Characters in anime productions vary widely in stature (from petite $1.45\text{m}$ schoolgirls to $1.75\text{m}$ tall heroines). The armature builder dynamically computes bounding box heights and vertex centroids directly from Milestone 1 sub-meshes, adjusting all 78 joint positions and bone lengths proportionally while maintaining flat sole ground-contact at $Z = 0.000\text{m}$.

### 4.3 Bone Roll Formulation: Zero-Twist Fist Curling

In Blender's bone coordinate frame:
- Local $Y$-axis: Directed longitudinally from Head to Tail along $\vec{v}_1 = \frac{\text{tail} - \text{head}}{\|\text{tail} - \text{head}\|}$.
- Local $Z$-axis: Directed dorsal (normal to the fingernail / extensor surface).
- Local $X$-axis: Directed laterally along the revolute hinge flexion axis ($X = Y \times Z$).

#### Mathematical Hinge Plane Normal Formulation:
For each finger defined by sequential joint vertices $P_0, P_1, P_2, P_3$:
1. Bone direction vectors:
   $$\vec{v}_{01} = \frac{P_1 - P_0}{\|P_1 - P_0\|}, \quad \vec{v}_{12} = \frac{P_2 - P_1}{\|P_2 - P_1\|}, \quad \vec{v}_{23} = \frac{P_3 - P_2}{\|P_3 - P_2\|}$$
2. The anatomical hinge revolute axis $\vec{h}$ is the normal to the planar flexion trajectory:
   $$\vec{h} = \frac{\vec{v}_{01} \times \vec{v}_{12}}{\|\vec{v}_{01} \times \vec{v}_{12}\|}$$
3. The dorsal normal up-vector $\vec{u}_{finger}$ is:
   $$\vec{u}_{finger} = \vec{h} \times \vec{v}_{01}$$
   With condition: if $\vec{u}_{finger} \cdot \hat{z} < 0$, set $\vec{u}_{finger} \leftarrow -\vec{u}_{finger}$ to guarantee it points dorsal/upward.
4. For Thumb, anatomical opposition requires a slightly medial-anterior dorsal vector:
   $$\vec{u}_{thumb} = \frac{(\text{sign} \cdot 0.5, -0.6, 0.6)}{\|(\text{sign} \cdot 0.5, -0.6, 0.6)\|}$$
5. Applying `edit_bone.align_roll(vec_up)` aligns local $Z$ with $\vec{u}_{finger}$ and guarantees that local $X$ is collinear with the anatomical hinge axis $\vec{h}$.

#### Fist Curl Behavior:
Rotating about local $X$ by angle $+\theta$:
$$\frac{\partial P_{tip}}{\partial \theta} = X \times Y = -Z$$
Since $-Z$ points directly toward the palmar center, **positive rotation around local $X$ curls every finger directly into the palm** with zero diagonal wobble or axis twisting.

## 5. Dynamic Landmark Scaling Formulation ($1.45\text{m} - 1.75\text{m}$)

To allow the rig to adapt automatically to characters of varying heights:
1. **Height Scale Detection**:
   Given character sub-meshes, calculate:
   $$Z_{min} = \min_{v \in \mathcal{V}} v_z, \quad Z_{max} = \max_{v \in \mathcal{V}} v_z$$
   $$H = Z_{max} - Z_{min}$$
   $$s = \frac{H}{1.650}, \quad Z_{sole} = Z_{min}$$
2. **Landmark Transformation**:
   For any canonical landmark $L = (x_0, y_0, z_0)$ defined at $1.650\text{m}$ height:
   $$L'(s) = \left(x_0 \cdot s, \; y_0 \cdot s, \; (z_0 - Z_{sole,0}) \cdot s + Z_{sole}\right)$$
3. **Vertex Centroid Refinement**:
   If submeshes are present in the scene, dynamic slices calculate the centroid of local rings (e.g. ankles, knees, wrists) to align bone heads exactly with the mesh centerline:
   $$\vec{C}_{joint} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}_{slice}|} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{slice}} \vec{v}$$
